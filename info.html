Understanding mental health

What mental health is, why it matters, how to manage stress and when to reach out. Then test yourself with a five-question quiz.

What is mental health?

Mental health includes our emotional, psychological and social wellbeing. It affects how we think, feel and act, and it shapes how we handle stress, relate to others and make choices.

Good mental health strengthens your ability to build fulfilling relationships, work productively, adapt to change and cope with the stresses of life.

Why it matters

Poor mental health can lead to physical health problems, substance misuse and lower productivity. Recognising the importance of mental wellbeing helps people seek help early and live healthier lives.

Investing in mental health also improves community wellbeing and reduces the social and economic burden of mental illness.

Know the signs, know your options

Tap a topic to expand it. If any of this sounds familiar, you don't have to work it out alone — see who you can talk to.

  • Depression: a persistent feeling of sadness or loss of interest.
  • Anxiety: excessive worry or fear that gets in the way of daily activities.
  • Burnout: emotional exhaustion caused by prolonged stress.
  • Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD): ongoing distress after a traumatic event.
  • Bipolar disorder: mood swings ranging from depressive lows to manic highs.

If you or someone you know has symptoms of these conditions, professional help is highly recommended.

  • Practise deep breathing or meditation — try the breathing exercise.
  • Take regular breaks from screens and work.
  • Exercise regularly and eat a balanced diet.
  • Talk to someone you trust.
  • Keep a consistent sleep schedule.
  • Set realistic goals and prioritise tasks.

Small daily habits can make a big difference to stress over time.

If you're feeling overwhelmed, hopeless or unable to function in daily life, talk to a mental health professional. Early support makes a big difference.

Other signs you may need help include:

  • Persistent mood changes or irritability
  • Withdrawing from social activities and loved ones
  • Thoughts of self-harm or suicide
  • Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
  • Changes in sleeping or eating patterns

Seeking help is a sign of strength. Find a helpline — many are free and open 24 hours.

Looking ahead

The future of mental health care

New technology and approaches are making support more accessible, personal and proactive.

💬

Therapy apps & chatbots

Digital tools offer guided exercises and immediate support between sessions.

🥽

Virtual reality

Immersive exposure therapy for anxiety and PTSD is becoming more common.

⌚

Wearables

Devices that track stress signals such as heart rate can prompt earlier support.

💻

Teletherapy

Remote sessions with professionals increase reach and convenience.

📊

Data analytics

Patterns in health data can help services spot people at risk sooner.

🧬

Personalised treatment

Care plans increasingly reflect each person's biology, history and environment.
